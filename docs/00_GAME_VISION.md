# 00 — Creative Director Brief / GAME VISION

## One-sentence hook
**BAD OMEN:** An agile black cat provokes a terrifyingly theatrical old witch into demolishing her own house; the player uses her temper as the world's biggest destruction tool.

**Tagline:** ONE CAT. TWO FLOORS. NO DEPOSIT REFUNDS.

**Format:** 3D third-person single-player short arcade physics sandbox, 7–10 min first run; immediate replay for a higher score. Desktop mouse + keyboard, browser/WebGL playable, suitable for jam judges to understand within 40 seconds.

## Origin and meaningful Komur role
Komur (Turkish for 'coal') is the signature black cat with white paws, chest mark, white-tipped tail, and amber eyes. He is the **playable hero, driver of every event, core animation focus, comic narrator, and cause of the Bad Omen prophecy**. Other cats are an optional reward/collectible, never a replacement for Komur. His small size, springy jumps, quick paws and mischievous meow unlock the gameplay. The phrase 'BAD OMEN' plays on the superstition that black cats bring bad luck while showing the home's owner causes her own catastrophes.

## Three pillars
1. **PLAY AS A CAT** — tight accelerating sprint, responsive small hops and huge leap, cat-sized navigational affordances, mantle, hide/scratch/swipe/taunt, readable close camera.
2. **PROVOKE THE MONSTER** — Grandma hears noise, investigates, hunts, throws objects, gets irrationally powerful as anger rises. The player aims her attacks at vulnerable surroundings.
3. **DESTRUCTION WITH COMEDIC PAYOFF** — satisfying small physics interactions escalates to authored multi-stage collapses, fire, shattered rooms, brief cinematic emphasis and a beautiful disastrous aftermath.

## Story in five beats
1. **Cold open (8–12s):** In a suspiciously charming, stormy walled cottage, an old woman counts charms and mutters, 'They say black cats bring bad luck...' A black tail brushes past a creaking door. Komur enters. Grandma yells, 'I CAUGHT YOU! You're not leaving until I get my hands on you!' Cat thought: 'Sounds like a challenge.' Start playable immediately.
2. **Petty crimes:** Dish racks, curtains and teacups. Grandma stomps around, getting angrier with every impact. Tutorial teaches swat, jump, taunt and hide.
3. **Learning the real weapon:** Grandma hurls a heavy object at a taunting cat; it smashes a cabinet or weak beam. Komur: 'Wow. She's better at this than me.' New objectives hint at 'bait the throw'.
4. **The omen escalates:** Kitchen catches a controlled stylized fire; upstairs debris crashes through a cracked section; raging Grandma storms across floors. Her witchy decorations rattle and the house groans.
5. **Finale:** With sufficiently cracked supports + max Rage, Grandma makes a desperate enormous swing/throw that breaks the final brace. Roof and props tumble in a brief camera sequence. Everyone escapes safely. Grandma glares at Komur on a surviving chair: 'I KNEW THAT CAT WAS BAD LUCK!' Komur: 'Some people never take responsibility.' Score screen.

## Tone
Cartoon-dark comedy, a little eerie, never cruel. Think Victorian cottage meets witch's lair: raven silhouette, crooked portraits, old wall clocks, teal moonlight, honey candle/fire glow, occasional amethyst/green magical sparks. No modern neon cyberpunk city required; retain some punchy stylized glow in magenta/violet runes as a nod to the requested aesthetic, not a sci-fi environment.

**Grandma is frightening to chase the cat, not a victim to injure.** She can hurl cartoon-sized spears/axes; collisions knock down house parts, not cause gore. Cat is caught and dramatically tossed into a safe soft spot (brief setback), not killed. Grandma evacuates any fire / final collapse.

## Emotional beats
- First 20s: delight at rapid movement and first smash.
- By 1m: laugh at Grandma's theatrical overreaction and first heavy throw.
- By 3m: realize you can weaponize Grandma for huge structural damage.
- By 5m: chain reaction cinematic rewards genuine planning.
- Finale: absurd crescendo with a punchline and scored report card.

## Winning criteria, translated to deliverables
- **Cat rule:** prominent Komur on title screen, intro, all gameplay, end scene, gameplay video.
- **Gameplay/fun:** 1-minute tutorial woven into action, expressive player physics, Granny bait skill loop, score pursuit.
- **Creativity:** the homeowner is the tool; bad omen means feedback loop between superstition and destruction.
- **Technical:** stable physics, animation blending, authored destruction, AI/line of sight, clean browser build.
- **Visual/audio:** distinct witchy house, high-impact particles and slow-mo cutaways, lots of specific voice reactions.

## Minimum game that is worthy of submission
Two floors + enclosed garden (can be compact), cat controller, Grandma state machine and 3 heavy attacks, 30 breakable items, 3 triggered chain reactions, one fire room, one final collapse, score/Rage/combos, subtitles, menu/ending. Polished encounters matter more than unbounded realism.

## Out-of-scope unless all gates pass
Open-world exterior, realistic continuous structural engineering, true fluid simulation, path-traced graphics, ten AI cats, deep inventory, multiplayer, save games, crafting, complex boss combat, 50 full authored cutscenes, fully motion-captured cat moves. Do not create a survival game where cat dies from an axe; that is not the joke.
